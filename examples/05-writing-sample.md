# On Writing Tools

There is a particular kind of silence that good writing software gets out of the way of. The words arrive, the screen holds them, and nothing else asks for attention. Most editors fail this test not through any single flaw but through accumulation — one more toolbar, one more panel, one more notification dot, until the page is a cockpit.

The markdown crowd promised an escape: *just text*. But raw markdown has its own noise. Every `**` and `#` is a small piece of scaffolding left standing in the finished room. You stop seeing your sentences and start seeing their plumbing.

## The bet

The bet minidown makes is that you can have both — the file stays plain text forever, and the screen shows you the writing, not the wiring. The syntax is still there. Put your cursor on any line and it reappears, obedient and dim, ready to be edited.

> The typewriter is the only machine that has ever improved by becoming quieter.

Whether that bet pays off depends on a hundred small decisions: how fast the marks reveal, where the cursor lands when you click a rendered word, how a half-typed `**bold` behaves while the thought is still forming. None of them matter individually. All of them matter together.

## What to feel for

1. Type a heading and watch the `#` fold away when you move on.
2. Write a sentence with **emphasis** mid-flow — does the reveal interrupt you?
3. Come back to an old paragraph and click into the middle of a styled phrase.
4. Let a long paragraph wrap for several lines and check the rhythm of the leading.

- [ ] Does it disappear?
- [ ] Does it come back exactly when needed?
- [ ] Did you stop noticing?

If the third box gets checked, the tool works.
